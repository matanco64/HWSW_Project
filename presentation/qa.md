## Answers each speaker must give without notes

### Yuval (hardware)

1. **Q20/G07 — Does the hardware beat Rust?** No: grape at 19.46 MHz projects to ~139 ms, 1.66x over the original, level with optimized Python (1.03x) and ~15x slower than Rust's 9.53 ms (NB:380-385); the pyflate chain does its stage in 4.25 ms against Rust's 3.30 ms and ties end to end, ~171 vs 170.01 ms (PY:461-470). The cause is clock and residual, not cycles: 124 cycles/step is 6.4 us at 19.46 MHz vs 0.48 us natively, matching Rust needs ~260 MHz, and the 11.6 ms Python residual alone exceeds Rust (NB:386-390).
2. **G01 — Why all three miss 50 MHz.** grape is 2.6x short, mtf 1.33x, huffman 1.25x (HR:491); the limiters are the integrate-multiply operand path, the table build / symtab readback mux and the 256:1 CAM read-mux (HR:200-202, HR:491-493, CO:187-189). The fixes are pipelining changes deferred beyond the PPA stage, and none of the numbers is routed silicon (HR:91-93).
3. **Q23 — Are the frequencies measured?** They are post-CTS static timing at the typical corner, 1/(constraint - worst setup slack): grape 150 ns + 98.62 ns slack -> 51.38 ns (HR:122-124), huffman 40 ns + 14.94 ns confirmed by a met 27 ns run at 39.5 MHz (HR:240-242), mtf a met 27 ns run with +0.31 ns -> 26.69 ns (HR:312-315). grape's is a 2.9x extrapolation with no confirmation run and is the weakest number in the report (HR:125-132).
4. **R03/G04 — Two profilers, which f?** Huffman's fraction is 0.496 on the PRD's local cProfile (ceiling 1.98x) and ~0.40 on the VM py-spy profile (12.63 + 9.95 + 9.41 + 5.91 + 2.42% of 372 samples, ceiling 1.67x); the report uses the sampled one because cProfile's per-call overhead inflates the many tiny bit-reader calls (HR:258-269). Neither standalone ceiling is a report claim: the shipped figure is the chain at the Rust boundary, ~6.6x end to end (HR:270-273, HR:478-482).
5. **Q22/R07 — What is verified, and what was waived?** Regressions 9/9, 17/17, 16/16 and the chain 2/2, byte-exact over 336,184 bytes in 159,303 cycles with and without back-pressure (HR:446, HR:393-395); line/toggle 91.7/96.0, 90.4/90.3, 92.0/93.8 with toggle on the <= 4-bit control subset for huffman and mtf (HR:447, HR:454-456). The `coverage_off` pragmas exclude unreachable wide storage and ROMs (PT:524); mtf's raw toggle is 82.8% (9,784/11,820) against 93.8% (720/768) on the subset, documented in the waivers (hw/mtf_cam/docs/coverage_waivers.md:15-16), and all seven mtf formal tasks pass with PASS committed (HR:487-490).
6. **R10 — What does the unmeasured T_if cost?** For grape it is ~150 AXI-Lite transactions once per 20,000-step run (70 body + 10 pair + 4 config in; 60 state + 3 counter out), under 0.03% of compute (HR:153-155). For pyflate the DMA of 67,562 B in and 336,184 B out is not measured and can only make the hardware rows slower; no run of Python attached to hardware exists (PY:447-448, PY:472-479).

### Matan (software)

1. **G10/Q16 — How were the numbers measured, and what does +- mean?** Course VM, pyperf, 120 values per configuration pinned to guest CPU 0 at revision 2c8c754 (PY:65-67), where the 120 values are 40 worker processes x 3 (AP:178). Mean +- SD describes spread, not a confidence interval: nbody's ICC of 0.99 / 0.93 gives an effective sample of ~40-42 and a standard error ~1.7x the naive one (AP:197-199).
2. **Q02/Q03 — Is specialization cheating?** It hard-codes the fixed pair schedule, not the trajectory: every force is still computed, and the state is bit-identical to the original after 20,000 steps (NB:164), giving 231.20 -> 143.13 ms, 1.62x, 38.1% (NB:35). The generator is checked at several N and falls back to a rolled loop above 20,000 pairs (NB:225).
3. **R09 — No timing on the submitted revision.** The timed revision is 2c8c754; later commits trimmed comments, moved the BWT histogram to collections.Counter and lowered the Rust code-length cap from 23 to 20, pass the same byte-exact checks and were not re-timed (PY:68-70; NB:174). bwt_transform is 25.41% of optimized self time (PY:190), so the Counter change is the one that could move 281.16 ms (PY:49); re-timing on the VM is the open item.
4. **R05 — 122 samples and you print 32.79%?** The optimized pyflate profile has 122 samples (PY:187), so 32.79% is a hotspot locator, not a calibrated fraction (PY:313-314). The speedup evidence is the 120-value end-to-end pair, 283.88 -> 170.01 ms, 1.67x (PY:272-273).
5. **Q10/Q15 — Why optimize more than the matcher, and does the profile explain 1.67x?** Instrumentation showed the matcher visiting ~5 of 258 entries on average (PY:83-84), and the VM ablation puts regex RLE4 at ~100 ms, the primary lookup at ~50 ms and counting-sort BWT at ~20 ms, trials agreeing within 4 ms (PY:181-183). The 32.79% share would cap the gain at 1.49x, while 1.67x needs at least 40.1% offloadable work; the derived 117 ms stage, ~41% of 283.88 ms, is consistent with that bound (PY:313-318).
6. **Q06 — Lower IPC but faster?** Native executes 26.7x fewer instructions and 14.9x fewer cycles while IPC falls from 3.28 to 1.83 (NB:210, NB:215). Runtime is cycles over clock, not IPC; interpreter bookkeeping retires efficiently and is still work that the native tier removes.
